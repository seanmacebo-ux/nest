💸 Money

Money follows a few simple rules: interest, inflation and fees. Learn those, and banks, car dealers and influencers lose their edge over you. Everything here uses South African figures checked in November 2026.

🧠 Understand it

1How money moves6 key points
  • Interest is the price of borrowing money. When you borrow, you pay it. When you save, you earn it.
  • Compound interest means you earn interest on your interest. R1,000 a month at 10% a year grows to about R759,000 after 20 years. You only put in R240,000.
  • Inflation (prices rising over time) quietly eats cash. Money sitting in a 0% account loses buying power every year.
  • Banks make money on the spread (the gap between what they pay savers and what they charge borrowers), plus fees.
  • The repo rate is the rate the Reserve Bank (SARB) charges banks. Prime is the base rate banks lend at, normally repo plus 3.5 points.
  • In September 2026 the SARB raised repo to 7.25%, so prime went to 10.75%. Most home and car loans are priced as prime plus or minus something, so your instalment moves when the SARB moves.
2Debt: friend or trap6 key points
  • Good debt buys something that grows or earns, like a home or a skill. Bad debt buys things that lose value fast, like clothes on store credit.
  • Your credit score is a number bureaus (companies that keep your borrowing history) give you. It drives the interest rate a bank offers you.
  • By law you get a free credit report from each credit bureau every year. TransUnion and Experian both offer this online.
  • If your report has a mistake, you can dispute it with the bureau that issued it, for free.
  • Paying on time every month matters most. Keep credit card and store card balances low compared to their limits.
  • The National Credit Act (NCA) is the law that governs lending. Only borrow from lenders registered with the National Credit Regulator (NCR).
3Growing money6 key points
  • Step one is an emergency fund (cash for surprises, like a job loss or a car repair). Aim for 3 to 6 months of expenses in a separate savings account.
  • A Tax-Free Savings Account (TFSA) lets your money grow with no tax on interest, dividends or growth. From 1 March 2026 the limit is R46,000 a year and R500,000 in your lifetime, per person.
  • Go over the TFSA limit and SARS charges 40% tax on the excess. Unused yearly space is lost, it does not carry over.
  • A retirement annuity (RA, a personal retirement fund) is tax-deductible. You can deduct up to 27.5% of your income, capped at R430,000 a year from March 2026.
  • Under the two-pot system (since September 2024), one-third of new retirement contributions goes to a savings pot. You can withdraw from it once per tax year, minimum R2,000, taxed at your marginal rate (the tax rate on your top slice of income).
  • Low-cost index funds and ETFs (exchange-traded funds, which buy a whole market in one go) beat most expensive funds over time. Fees matter: R1,000 a month for 30 years grows to about R1.83m at 9% a year, but only R1.35m at 7.5%.
4Housing: rent vs buy6 key points
  • Renting is not 'dead money'. A bond also has dead money: interest, rates, levies, insurance and repairs.
  • A R1.2m bond over 20 years at prime (10.75%) costs about R12,180 a month. Compare that full cost to your rent, not just the price tag.
  • Transfer duty (a tax on buying property) is 0% on homes up to R1,210,000 for 2026/27. You still pay bond registration and conveyancing (legal transfer) fees.
  • A deposit lowers your loan and often gets you a better rate. Banks check your income, expenses, credit record and existing debts.
  • Bond originators like ooba and BetterBond send one application to several banks so they compete for you. ooba's service is free to you.
  • First Home Finance (formerly FLISP) is a once-off government subsidy for first-time buyers with household income of R3,501 to R22,000 a month. If you earn above that together, you will not qualify.
5Cars and car loans6 key points
  • A car loses value from day one. Treat it as a cost, not an investment.
  • Vehicle finance uses the car as security, so it is usually cheaper than an unsecured personal loan.
  • Small rate gaps add up. A R250,000 car over 72 months costs about R4,730 a month at 10.75%, and about R4,990 at 12.75%. That gap is roughly R19,000 over the loan.
  • A balloon payment (a big lump sum you owe at the end) lowers the monthly instalment but costs more. With a 30% balloon at 12.75%, the instalment drops to about R4,290, but the total rises to about R384,000.
  • Total cost of ownership is instalment plus insurance, fuel, services, tyres and tolls. Budget for all of it before you choose a car.
  • A bigger deposit, a shorter term, no balloon and a clean credit report are what get you a better deal by Q2 2027.
6Building your own thing6 key points
  • Side income is taxable. SARS counts it together with your salary.
  • For 2026/27, you pay no income tax under 65 if your total taxable income is below R99,000.
  • If you earn income that is not salary, SARS treats you as a provisional taxpayer. That means you pay tax in advance, by the end of August and the end of February.
  • Keep records of what you earn and spend on the side hustle. Legitimate business costs can reduce the tax you owe.
  • You can register a company online with CIPC (the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission). Start as a sole trader first if it is small, and register a company once it grows.
  • Grow skills and income first. A raise or a client is worth far more than chasing a few extra percent on savings.

✅ What we actually do

❓ Questions to ask

Screenshot these or read them out.

  1. What is the interest rate as prime plus or minus, and is it fixed or linked to prime?
  2. What is the total amount I will repay over the full term, including all fees?
  3. Is there a balloon or residual payment in this deal? Show me the quote without one.
  4. What is the monthly service fee and initiation fee, and are they included in the instalment?
  5. What credit life or insurance are you adding, and can I use my own cover instead?
  6. What are the total annual fees on this fund (TER plus platform plus advice fees)?
  7. Are you registered with the FSCA, and what is your FSP number?

⚠️ Watch out for

  • Forex and crypto 'mentors' selling courses or signals. Most earn from your fees and sign-ups, not from trading.
  • Guaranteed high returns are a lie. Real investments go up and down.
  • MLMs (multi-level marketing) where you profit by recruiting people, not by selling to real customers.
  • Deepfake videos of Ramaphosa, Motsepe or TV presenters promoting investments. The FSCA has warned about these.
  • Loan sharks (mashonisas) who want to keep your bank card or ID as security. Walk away.
  • High balloon payments that make a car look affordable. The lump sum does not go away.
  • Two-pot withdrawals for non-emergencies. You pay tax now and lose years of compound growth.
  • Paying a website for your credit report. You get one free from each bureau every year.
