What the research says, so you buy smart:

  • Plates + bowls: cheap is fine, with one rule. A 2023 SA Medical Research Council / UJ study tested 44 plates and bowls from Joburg chain stores. Most had lead in the glaze, but only one leached it into food (and it was SA-made). Branded shops aren't safer; SA doesn't test cookware for lead at all. The rule: plain white or clear on the eating surface, with no paint inside or on the rim, and no chips or cracks. Bright red, orange or yellow decoration is the risk (FDA).
  • Glasses: safe cheap, if plain. Skip painted designs.
  • Cutlery: cheap is OK. It's a rust and durability issue, not a safety one. The magnet test only rules out the very cheapest steel, so don't trust "18/10" or "SUS304" stamps either.
  • Non-stick: cheap is OK. Price doesn't predict how long the coating lasts (Choice's tests). With ANY brand: never heat it empty on high, and bin it once it's scratched.
  • Pots: pick ones with a heavy, thick base that sits flat. Thin ones scorch broth. Skip plain grey bare-aluminium pots, especially for tomato or acidic food.
  • Skip melamine for hot food, cheap or branded. Germany's food-safety agency (BfR) says no hot food in it. For baby stuff later: stainless, glass or silicone.
  • Ignore "lead-free" stickers and home lead-test swabs: swabs gave false "safe" results in 56 of 104 tests (US product-safety regulator, CPSC).

🍳 Branded options (25 Oct): if China Centre doesn't have it

Value kitchen: ~R8,240 (all the kitchen cards below, not counting the blenders). Step-up (Le Creuset, Tramontina, Corelle, 18/10 cutlery): ~R20,700. Spend on: the knife and the pots, because you use them every day. Save on: plates, glasses and cutlery. Corelle can wait until the toddler throws things. Le Creuset (R7,699) mostly buys a lifetime warranty. The Sagenwolf does the same soups at a fifth of the price. Get a stockpot for bone broth: a 5L cast-iron pot full of broth weighs ~9kg and is too small for bones.

Buy for move day (~R4.7k): pot set, plates, cutlery, glasses, both knives, board, spoons. Later: glass containers, Dutch oven, skillet, stockpot. Several Takealot items say 5–7 work days, so order by ~27 Oct to have them by 1 Nov. Cast iron works on any stove; on glass-ceramic, lift it, don't slide it.

🍲 Blender shortlist (25 Oct): picked for reliability, not watts

The honest take: for soups, broth soups, sauces and baby purée, a good stick blender is the best tool. You blend right in the pot, so there's no pouring of hot liquid, and baby portions are too small for a 2L jug. Add a jug blender only if smoothies or frozen fruit matter. Dropped: Genesis Nutrimax (Verimark brand, ~1.5★ on Hello Peter, the 2200W can't be verified) and the Russell Hobbs Nexus. Vitamix is the only true buy-it-for-life brand, but it's ~R12.7k in SA, out of stock, and has no local refurb programme.
