🏆 How the models rank

Scored on gearbox, parts, real fuel use and known problems, for automatics under R220k. Sources: Cars.co.za buyer's guides, CAR magazine and The Citizen, read 12 Nov.

1. Suzuki Vitara Brezza 1.5 auto (2021–22). Normal automatic, cheap shared Suzuki parts (123 dealers), 6.8 L/100km measured, not an Uber car. Catch: 2 airbags, no stability control.

2. Suzuki Vitara 1.6 auto (2016–19), GL+ only. The best engine and gearbox on the list, 6-speed normal auto. Hard to find in Gauteng right now. Skip the GLX: its glass roof eats headroom.

3. Toyota Corolla 1.8 CVT (2015–18). Cheapest to keep running, most space, parts at 220 Toyota dealers, R135–220k. Test-drive for CVT judder. None at the big dealer groups under R220k today, so this one means looking at smaller dealers.

4. Kia Rio 1.4, 6-speed auto (2019+). Simple and reliable. Avoid the older 4-speed.

5. Honda Jazz 1.5 CVT (2016–21). Best for your height (steering adjusts for reach), 5.8 L/100km claimed. Check the CVT's history.

Skip: Ford Fiesta auto (left SA in 2022, used 9.2 L/100km in a test, engine belt issue) · VW Polo DSG at this budget (high km on a fragile gearbox) · Hyundai Creta and Venue (steering doesn't adjust for reach, hard at 6'4) · Toyota Urban Cruiser (same car as the Brezza for ~R20k more) · Kia Rio and Hyundai i20 4-speed autos.

The one test at 6'4: no review tested a driver your height. Sit in it with the seat all the way back, then sit behind yourself. Knees clear front and back = pass.

🛡️ Buying safely

SAFE Franchise + dealer-group used stock: fixed price, a dealer you can go back to, usually a service history, and sometimes leftover factory warranty. Test drive before you sign. HUNT Burchmore's, bank repos + auctions: cheapest, but sold as-is with no warranty and no returns. Check the viewing date and inspect on site every time.

Before any money moves, on any car:

  • Check for outstanding finance (TransUnion/Lightstone, a few hundred rand). A financed car can be repossessed from you even after you've paid.
  • Independent inspection by the AA or an RMI workshop (~R1,000–R1,500).
  • VIN on the car matches the papers, and the NATIS reg doc is the original, not a copy.
  • Seller's ID matches the registered owner.
  • Ask for the on-the-road price. Some dealers add licensing, admin and delivery fees on top of the advertised price.
  • Never pay a deposit before seeing the car and papers in person. Rushed deal = walk away.

🏷️ The brands: reliability + resale
🇯🇵 Toyota Resale strong
The benchmark. Parts everywhere and cheap. Pay a premium up front, get it back at resale.
🇯🇵 Suzuki Resale strong
Simple, cheap to run and fix, few gremlins. The budget can't-go-wrong pick.
🇯🇵 Mazda Resale strong
A notch more premium for little reliability trade-off. Parts moderate.
🇯🇵 Honda Resale good
Durable and comfy. Thinner dealer network, so servicing takes more planning.
🇯🇵 Nissan Resale average
Solid now, but check the CVT gearbox service history on anything pre-2020.
🇯🇵 Mitsubishi Resale avg–strong
Underrated durability. Smaller network outside the metros.
🇰🇷 Kia Resale improving
No longer a budget risk. Long warranty that carries into the used years.
🇰🇷 Hyundai Resale good
The 7-yr/200,000km warranty is transferable, so you inherit real cover.
🇪🇺 Volkswagen Resale good
Great to drive. Budget for DSG gearbox + electrical repairs once the plan runs out.
🇪🇺 Ford Resale average
Engines fine. Avoid the pre-2018 PowerShift dual-clutch auto.
🇪🇺 Renault Resale weak–avg
Cheap to buy, but faster depreciation and electrical risk out of warranty.
🇪🇺 Peugeot Resale weak
Nice cabins, smallest network here. The 1.2 PureTech has a timing-belt weakness.
🇨🇳 Haval / GWM Resale strong (for Chinese)
Safest Chinese bet: 10+ yrs in SA, long warranty, cheaper parts.
🇨🇳 Chery / Omoda Resale avg–strong
Best warranty on paper (up to 10yr/1m km engine). Network still maturing.
🇨🇳 MG Resale weak–avg
Newest and least proven here. The long warranty is the insurance you want.
