💳 Paying for it: loan vs car finance

Redone 24 Nov with deposits, balloons, realistic rates and the FNB loan. Prime is 10.75% (Codex, 14 Nov). Rates are ranges, not quotes: the real rate only comes from the bank's offer. Every number includes the R1,207.50 start fee and R69 a month admin fee (the legal maximums).

Rates you'll realistically get. Car finance is secured by the car, so it's cheaper: about 12.75% with a strong credit record, about 14.75% for a first car with a short record, up to ~16.75%. A personal loan has nothing behind it: about 18% at best, about 24% likely, and 28.25% is the legal maximum. The tables use the likely rates.

The FNB loan you're already paying. Mishka's FNB loan is R5,000 a month for 12 months from Oct 2026, so it ends around Oct 2027. The car is about Q2 2027. If both run together, that's R5k plus R5–6.5k a month, on top of the new medical aid and gap cover (about R3.5–4.2k). Banks count the FNB loan when they decide how much to lend and at what rate. Clearing it first frees the R5k and gives Mishka a fully paid-up loan on her record, which is what earns a better rate.

Why a personal loan anyway: the car is yours from day one with no bank on the papers, you can sell it whenever, and you choose your own insurance. Why not: at likely rates it costs about R80k more on R270k. Dealers don't always discount cash buyers, because they earn commission on finance. And you'd still want comprehensive insurance on a new car.

Who applies: FNB and WesBank want SA citizens or permanent residents, so a pending spousal visa won't qualify. Standard Bank says temporary residents can ask at a branch. So Mishka would apply, and the bank looks at her income and her FNB loan. Credit life cover usually adds a few hundred rand a month on either; ask for it in the quote. Each full application is a credit check, so get one finance quote and one loan quote, not five.

Our lean: clear FNB first, save a cash deposit, then car finance with no balloon, 5 years or less, and pay extra every month. You own it outright at the end, for about half the interest of a personal loan.

📚 Before you buy

🛡️ Buying safely

SAFE Franchise + dealer-group used stock: fixed price, a dealer you can go back to, usually a service history, and sometimes leftover factory warranty. Test drive before you sign. HUNT Burchmore's, bank repos + auctions: cheapest, but sold as-is with no warranty and no returns. Check the viewing date and inspect on site every time.

Before any money moves, on any car:

  • Check for outstanding finance (TransUnion/Lightstone, a few hundred rand). A financed car can be repossessed from you even after you've paid.
  • Independent inspection by the AA or an RMI workshop (~R1,000–R1,500).
  • VIN on the car matches the papers, and the NATIS reg doc is the original, not a copy.
  • Seller's ID matches the registered owner.
  • Ask for the on-the-road price. Some dealers add licensing, admin and delivery fees on top of the advertised price.
  • Never pay a deposit before seeing the car and papers in person. Rushed deal = walk away.
