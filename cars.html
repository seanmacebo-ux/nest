💳 Paying for it: loan vs car finance

Rebuilt 24 Nov from the banks' own pages, the SARB and the NCR (sources at the bottom). Prime is 10.75%, repo 7.25% (SARB, unchanged on 19 Nov; next decision 28 Jan 2027). Every number includes the R1,207.50 start fee and R69 a month that every lender charges.

The real rates. Car finance: the banks' own calculators start at 13.00% (WesBank), 13.75% (Nedbank MFC, "prime + 3%") and 14.25% (Standard Bank). Dealer deals go lower but come with strings: Toyota 12.25% (10% deposit, 48 months, big final payment) and Suzuki 11.18% (40% balloon, "may increase depending on risk profile"). Legal maximum 24.25%. Personal loan: banks advertise "from" 12.50% (Capitec), 13.75% (Absa) and 15.15% (FNB), but those are for the best records. Nedbank's own page says South Africans pay 20% to 28.25% on average, and 28.25% is the legal maximum. Personal loans also force credit life cover: R2.58 to R4.50 per R1,000 owed, every month (Capitec, NCR cap). No bank publishes what a first-time borrower gets, so below we show both ends.

🆕 The in-between option: Capitec Vehicle Loan. A loan made for buying a car, but unsecured, so you own the car from day one with no bank on the papers (what you wanted on 14 Nov). Up to R500k over up to 84 months, interest "from prime" (10.75%) "based on your credit profile". Capitec doesn't publish the top of its range; because it's unsecured, the legal maximum is 28.25%. Worth getting a real quote next to a car-finance quote. Capitec's page ↗

Why people say a bank loan is easier, and they're right about this part: approval can be paid out in minutes (FNB), you own the car from day one, you can buy privately, and the lender doesn't dictate your insurance. It's the better deal only if the real quote comes in close to car finance. Being a "cash buyer" doesn't guarantee a discount: dealers earn commission on finance.

Costs that hide in a car deal: dealer extras (extended warranty, tyre and rim, scratch and dent, service-plan top-ups). Every R10,000 of extras financed adds about R228 a month. Ask for a written price breakdown and say no to extras you don't want. A 1% rise in the rate adds about R179 a month.

The real blocker is whose income counts. The R52k is Sean's salary. A bank won't automatically let Mishka borrow against it, and Sean's visa is pending. Car costs plus the FNB loan come to about R14,500 a month before fuel. Waiting until FNB is paid off frees R5,000 a month.

Ask every lender: 1) Whose income qualifies, and what's the total I'll repay in writing, with every fee, insurance and extra? 2) Is the rate fixed or does it move with prime, and can we use our own insurance? 3) How are extra payments applied, and what does settling early cost?

Paying early is free on loans up to R250k. Above R250k (both of these), the law lets the lender charge up to 3 months' interest as an early-settlement fee. FNB and Nedbank say they don't charge it on personal loans; ask for it in writing on any quote.

The FNB loan you're already paying. Mishka's FNB loan is R5,000 a month for 12 months from Oct 2026, so it ends around Oct 2027. The car is about Q2 2027. Banks count it when they decide how much to lend and at what rate. Clearing it first frees the R5k and leaves a fully paid loan on her record. That's the only thing we found that moves a first-time borrower towards the low end of these ranges: TransUnion and AutoTrader both say the rate is set by your credit score and history.

Who can apply. FNB personal loans: permanent SA residents only, employed or self-employed. Absa personal loans accept self-employed (tax assessment, 6 months of statements, accountant's letter). Standard Bank car finance needs an SA ID. Absa car finance accepts a work permit and passport. WesBank Graduate Finance needs no credit history if you're under 31 with a degree from the last 3 years. Car finance always requires comprehensive insurance (your own insurer is fine).

Our lean: clear FNB, save a cash deposit, then car finance with no balloon, 5 years or less, and pay extra every month. On the Urban Cruiser that's roughly R100k to R230k less than a personal loan over 5 years, and you still own it outright at the end.

📚 Before you buy

🛡️ Buying safely

SAFE Franchise + dealer-group used stock: fixed price, a dealer you can go back to, usually a service history, and sometimes leftover factory warranty. Test drive before you sign. HUNT Burchmore's, bank repos + auctions: cheapest, but sold as-is with no warranty and no returns. Check the viewing date and inspect on site every time.

Before any money moves, on any car:

  • Check for outstanding finance (TransUnion/Lightstone, a few hundred rand). A financed car can be repossessed from you even after you've paid.
  • Independent inspection by the AA or an RMI workshop (~R1,000–R1,500).
  • VIN on the car matches the papers, and the NATIS reg doc is the original, not a copy.
  • Seller's ID matches the registered owner.
  • Ask for the on-the-road price. Some dealers add licensing, admin and delivery fees on top of the advertised price.
  • Never pay a deposit before seeing the car and papers in person. Rushed deal = walk away.
