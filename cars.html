How to decide from here

The green "street" row is the real going rate (WeBuyCars/private). At your budget the sweet spot sits around R200k–R290k for a modern automatic. For a proper private deal, aim for the WeBuyCars price minus ~10–15% on Gumtree/Facebook for the same year + spec.

Best value plays right now: Ford EcoSport, Renault Duster, Honda BR-V (7-seat) and Suzuki Ertiga (7-seat) — soft resale or MPV space means you pay well under dealer. Skip if budget's tight: Toyota Yaris Cross (least discounted, the "Toyota tax").

The one test at 6'4: sit in it with the seat all the way back, fit a rear-facing seat behind you. Knees clear + seat fits = pass. Send me the models you like (tap 📷) and I'll narrow to a final 3–4 to go view.

⚠️ Buying private (no dealer warranty) — protect yourself

Private (Gumtree/FB) is cheapest but there's zero comeback if something's wrong. Before any money changes hands:

  • Check for outstanding finance (TransUnion/Lightstone history check, a few hundred rand) — a financed car can be repossessed from you even after you've paid.
  • Independent inspection — AA or an RMI workshop (~R1,000–R1,500) before you buy.
  • VIN on the car matches the papers, and the NATIS reg doc is an original, not a copy.
  • Seller's ID matches the registered owner (or a clear paper trail for estate/company sales).
  • Meet in daylight, public place. Never pay a deposit before seeing the car + papers in person. Rushed deal = walk away.

🏢 The brands — before you commit

Reliability, warranty, parts and resale in SA. The Chinese brands aren't a blanket risk anymore — it's brand-by-brand now, and Haval is the one with real evidence behind it.

🇯🇵 Toyota Resale strong
The benchmark. Parts everywhere and cheap. Pay a premium up front, get it back at resale. The safe default.
🇯🇵 Suzuki Resale strong
Simple, cheap to run and fix, few gremlins. Punches way above its price. The budget can't-go-wrong pick.
🇯🇵 Mazda Resale strong
A notch more premium for little reliability trade-off. Parts moderate. CX models hold value well.
🇯🇵 Honda Resale good
Durable and comfy. Thinner dealer network, so servicing takes a bit more planning.
🇯🇵 Nissan Resale average
Solid now, but check the CVT gearbox service history carefully on anything pre-2020.
🇯🇵 Mitsubishi Resale avg–strong
Underrated durability (Xpander/Pajero). Smaller network outside the metros.
🇰🇷 Kia Resale improving
No longer a budget risk. Long warranty that actually protects you into the used-car years.
🇰🇷 Hyundai Resale good
The 7-yr/200,000km warranty is transferable — you inherit real cover, not just a used car. Big win.
🇪🇺 Volkswagen Resale good
Great to drive, but budget for DSG gearbox + electrical repairs once the plan runs out. That's the trap.
🇪🇺 Ford Resale average
Engines fine, but confirm the gearbox on pre-2018 autos — the PowerShift dual-clutch is a known landmine.
🇪🇺 Renault Resale weak–avg
Cheap to buy, but faster depreciation and French electrical risk once out of warranty.
🇪🇺 Peugeot Resale weak
Nice cabins, but the smallest network here — resale and confidence reflect that.
🇨🇳 Haval / GWM Resale strong (for Chinese)
Safest Chinese bet: 10+ yrs in SA, long warranty, parts 30–40% cheaper than German. Resale now backs it up.
🇨🇳 Chery / Omoda Resale avg–strong
Best warranty on paper (up to 10yr/1m km engine). Newer Tiggo Pro range is good; network still maturing.
🇨🇳 MG Resale weak–avg
Newest and least proven here. The long warranty is exactly the insurance you want given the short track record.

Bottom line: none of the Chinese brands carry Toyota-level resale certainty yet, but "risky Chinese car" is outdated — Haval has the most evidence behind it, Chery/Omoda the best warranty, MG the thinnest track record.
