🌙 Mattress — what to look for (Mishka's comfort first)

She'll side-sleep more as the pregnancy goes on, so the bed matters more than anything else in the room. The checklist:

  • Firmness: medium / medium-plush. Too firm = hip + shoulder pressure (numb arm, tossing). Too soft = the back sinks and lower-back pain gets worse. Medium-plush is the pregnancy sweet spot.
  • Type: pocket-spring or hybrid. Avoid Bonnell (connected) springs — movement carries across the bed. Avoid pure memory foam — it sleeps hot (bad in a Joburg summer, and pregnancy runs warm).
  • Comfort layer: pillow-top or zoned foam — soft under hips + shoulders, firmer under the lower back. That's the "sink-in without sinking" feel.
  • Cooling: gel / coolgel top layer — keeps her from overheating.
  • Motion isolation: pocket coils so you don't wake each other (her sleep's already lighter with bathroom trips + later the baby).
  • Edge support: reinforced edges make getting in and out easier with a bump.
  • Base: match the brand. Most warranties only hold if the mattress sits on the same-brand base — a mismatched or old base can void a sag claim.
  • Size: consider a king or queen-XL. More room as the bump grows (pillows between the knees), and space if the baby co-sleeps early on.
  • Trial > showroom. Buy where there's a real 100-night in-home trial — her ideal firmness will change by 32 weeks, and 5 minutes in a shop won't show it.
  • 💡 Cheapest big win: a pregnancy / body pillow (R300–R900) often does more for sleep than the mattress — it supports the bump and keeps the top leg raised.

Best all-rounder for her: the Restonic Pocket Cool (pocket springs + zoned foam + gel + 100-night trial). Safer brand/retailer alternative: Sealy Hybrid Sage Plush. Go lie on both together, then lean on the trial.
